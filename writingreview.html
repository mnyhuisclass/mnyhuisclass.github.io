📖 Source: Guardians of the Kelp Forest

Paragraph 1: Sea otters are marine mammals that live in the North Pacific Ocean. While most marine mammals like whales and seals have a thick layer of fat called blubber to keep them warm, the sea otter is different. It relies entirely on its unique fur to survive in the freezing water.

Paragraph 2: This fur is the densest in the animal kingdom. A sea otter has about one million hairs per square inch! To put that in perspective, a human only has about 100,000 hairs on their entire head. This thick coat traps a thin layer of air right next to the otter's skin, acting like a waterproof diving suit.

Paragraph 3: Because their fur is so important for warmth, otters spend many hours every day grooming. They use their paws and teeth to clean their fur and blow air bubbles back into it. If the fur gets dirty or matted, it can no longer trap air, and the otter could quickly freeze to death.

Paragraph 4: Sea otters are also famous for being one of the few animals that use tools. They are clever hunters that dive to the ocean floor to find food. Since many of their favorite snacks are hidden inside hard shells, they have developed a special way to get to the meat inside.

Paragraph 5: When an otter finds a clam or a crab, it also looks for a flat, sturdy rock. It tucks the rock and the food into a loose flap of skin under its front leg, which acts like a built-in pocket. Once it returns to the surface, the otter floats on its back and places the rock on its chest.

Paragraph 6: Floating comfortably, the otter holds the shellfish with both paws and smashes it against the rock. This repetitive pounding breaks the hard shell, allowing the otter to reach its meal. Scientists believe this behavior is passed down from mothers to their pups through careful teaching.

Paragraph 7: Keeping warm and active requires an incredible amount of energy. To maintain their body temperature, sea otters have a very high metabolism. This means their bodies burn through fuel very quickly, much like a race car burns through gasoline during a long race.

Paragraph 8: Due to this high energy need, an adult sea otter must eat a huge amount of food. In a single day, an otter can eat up to 25% of its own body weight! For a human, that would be like eating 50 hamburgers in one afternoon. They hunt for sea urchins, abalone, and various types of fish.

Paragraph 9: Sea otters are known as a "keystone species." This means they play a vital role in keeping their environment healthy. They love to eat sea urchins, which eat kelp. If there were no otters, the urchins would eat all the kelp, and the underwater "forest" where many fish live would disappear.

Paragraph 10: Today, sea otters face many challenges, such as ocean pollution and oil spills. However, because they are protected by law, their populations are slowly growing again. By studying these intelligent creatures, researchers hope to learn more about how to protect our oceans for the future.
